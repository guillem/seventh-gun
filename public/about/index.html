About Seventh Gun

A seeded, late-1990s-style first-person shooter that runs entirely in the browser. Every maze, texture, sound and enemy is generated from a seed; the seven campaign maps are authored by hand.

The idea

Seventh Gun is a shooter in the Wolfenstein and Doom lineage, built for a browser tab instead of a CD. There is nothing to install, no account, no launcher, no asset download: the whole game is about 250 kB over the wire and everything you see is computed at load time from a short text seed.

The seed is the point. Type nightjar on Normal and you get one maze; type it again tomorrow, on another machine, and you get the exact same rooms, the same enemy placement, the same loot. That turns a run into something you can hand to another person as a word, and it turns the game into a small roguelike rather than a fixed campaign you memorise.

On top of that sits an authored campaign of seven maps, each with its own name, palette and voice, where the guns you pick up stay with you and your health resets. A separate arena mode runs small-team deathmatch on a generated 96×96 grid for up to ten players.

The seven guns

Each gun changes how you fight rather than just how much damage you deal. You start with the pistol and pick the rest up along the route; the seventh is always waiting in the antechamber, and picking it up breaks the seal on the finale arena.

Two builds, one game

There are two deployments of the same game. seventhgun.com is the classic look. art.seventhgun.com is an experimental art build with a different rendering treatment, kept as a separate Worker and deployed from its own branch. Gameplay, seeds and maps are the same; if you are writing about the game, refer to the classic build and mention the experimental one as an alternative look.

What it is not

No accounts, no telemetry, no analytics pipeline, no store. No mobile app. No paid tier. The source is MIT-licensed, so running your own copy, including the optional Node or Docker arena server, is supported and documented in the repository.
