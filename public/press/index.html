Descriptions

One line

A seeded, late-1990s-style first-person shooter that runs entirely in the browser, where a short word is the whole maze.

Fifty words

Seventh Gun is a retro first-person shooter that runs in a browser tab with nothing to install. Every maze, texture, sound and enemy comes from a seed, so a run is a word you can hand to someone else. Seven guns, seven authored campaign maps, and an optional multiplayer arena.

One hundred and fifty words

Seventh Gun is a first-person shooter in the Wolfenstein and Doom lineage, built for the browser instead of a disc. There is no install, no account and no asset download: the game computes its mazes, textures, sounds and enemies from a short text seed, which means the same seed always rebuilds the same run on any machine. That makes a run shareable as a word and turns the game into a small roguelike rather than a fixed campaign to memorise. You start with a pistol and find six more guns along a spine of rooms, each one changing how you fight rather than just how hard you hit; the seventh shatters the seal on the finale arena. Alongside the seeded mode sits a seven-map authored campaign, a built-in map editor with shareable map codes, and an optional ten-player arena. It is free, MIT-licensed, and runs on desktop or mobile.

Contact

Issues and questions: github.com/guillem/seventh-gun/issues. That is the fastest route and the one that reaches the developer directly.

What makes it different

  • No install and no store: one URL, playable in seconds on a phone.
  • Deterministic seeds, so runs are reproducible and shareable as text.
  • Guns with identity rather than stat tiers: a piercing beam, an arcing grenade that hurts you too, a void cannon that can erase a whole pack.
  • A real map editor with compact share codes, not a level-select screen.
  • MIT-licensed, self-hostable, no telemetry and no accounts.
